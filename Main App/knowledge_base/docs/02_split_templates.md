# Workout Split Templates by Training Days per Week

## Choosing a split
The best split is the one that hits each muscle group about twice per week with 10-20 weekly hard sets, fits the person's schedule, and leaves at least 48 hours before the same muscle is trained hard again. Training fewer than 3 days per week makes it hard to reach effective frequency, so 3 days is the minimum recommended.

## 3 days per week: Full Body (A/B/C)
Best for beginners, busy schedules, and cutting phases. Each session trains the whole body with one exercise per major movement pattern. Schedule example: Monday, Wednesday, Friday with rest days in between.
- Day A: squat pattern, horizontal push (bench press or push-up), horizontal pull (row), hamstring/hinge accessory, biceps or core.
- Day B: hip hinge (Romanian deadlift), vertical push (overhead press), vertical pull (pull-up or lat pulldown), lunge pattern, triceps.
- Day C: squat or leg press variation, incline push, row variation, lateral raises, biceps curls, core.
Each muscle is trained 3 times per week with 3-5 sets per session, giving 9-15 weekly sets.

## 4 days per week: Upper / Lower
Excellent for beginners moving to intermediate, and for bulking or maintenance. Schedule example: Monday Upper A, Tuesday Lower A, Thursday Upper B, Friday Lower B.
- Upper A (strength bias): bench press, barbell row, overhead press, lat pulldown, biceps curl, triceps extension.
- Lower A (squat bias): back squat, Romanian deadlift, walking lunges, leg curl, calf raise, plank.
- Upper B (hypertrophy bias): incline dumbbell press, chest-supported row, dumbbell shoulder press, pull-ups, lateral raises, hammer curls.
- Lower B (hinge bias): deadlift or hip thrust, leg press or goblet squat, Bulgarian split squat, leg extension, calf raise, hanging knee raise.

## 5 days per week: Upper / Lower + Push / Pull / Legs (hybrid)
Suited to intermediate lifters who want more volume, commonly used for bulking. Schedule example: Monday Upper, Tuesday Lower, Wednesday rest, Thursday Push, Friday Pull, Saturday Legs, Sunday rest. Each muscle is hit twice per week.
- Upper: compound press, compound row, vertical press, vertical pull, arms.
- Lower: squat, hinge, single-leg work, calves.
- Push: chest, shoulders, triceps (incline press, shoulder press, flyes, lateral raises, triceps pushdowns).
- Pull: back and biceps (pull-ups, rows, face pulls, curls).
- Legs: quad and glute emphasis (squat variation, hip thrust, lunges, leg curl).

## 6 days per week: Push / Pull / Legs x2
For intermediate and advanced lifters with good recovery, sleep and nutrition. Schedule: Push, Pull, Legs, Push, Pull, Legs, Rest. The first rotation can bias heavier loads (5-8 reps) and the second rotation moderate loads (8-15 reps). Keep per-session volume moderate (14-20 sets) because frequency is high. Not recommended for true beginners, who generally recover better and progress just as fast with 3-4 days.

## Matching split to goal
- Bulking: 4-6 days, higher volume (14-20 sets per muscle per week), focus on progressive overload in moderate rep ranges.
- Cutting: 3-5 days, keep intensity (load) high to preserve muscle, reduce volume by about 20-30% relative to a bulk, add daily steps or low-intensity cardio.
- Maintenance / recomposition: 3-5 days, moderate volume (10-14 sets per muscle per week); about one-third of normal volume is enough to maintain muscle if intensity is kept high (Bickel et al., 2011).

## Matching split to experience
- Beginner (under 1 year of consistent training): full body 3 days or upper/lower 4 days, 2-3 sets per exercise, focus on technique.
- Intermediate (1-3 years): upper/lower, hybrid, or PPL, 3-4 sets per exercise.
- Advanced (3+ years): PPL x2 or specialization blocks, periodized intensity.
