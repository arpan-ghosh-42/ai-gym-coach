# Training and Nutrition by Goal: Bulking, Cutting, Maintenance

## Estimating energy needs
The Mifflin-St Jeor equation estimates basal metabolic rate (BMR):
- Men: BMR = 10 x weight(kg) + 6.25 x height(cm) - 5 x age + 5
- Women: BMR = 10 x weight(kg) + 6.25 x height(cm) - 5 x age - 161
Total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) = BMR x activity factor: 1.375 for 3 training days per week, 1.465 for 4, 1.55 for 5, 1.725 for 6 or more. These are estimates; adjust based on 2-3 weeks of scale-weight trend.

## Protein intake
A meta-analysis by Morton et al. (2018, British Journal of Sports Medicine) found that protein intakes up to about 1.6 g per kg of body weight per day maximize gains from resistance training, with an upper confidence bound near 2.2 g/kg. During a calorie deficit, higher intakes of 2.0-2.4 g/kg help preserve lean mass (Helms et al., 2014). Spread protein across 3-5 meals of 0.3-0.5 g/kg each.

## Bulking (muscle gain)
- Calorie surplus: roughly 250-500 kcal above maintenance (about 10-15%). Larger surpluses mostly add fat.
- Target rate of gain: about 0.25-0.5% of body weight per week (Iraki et al., 2019). Beginners can gain toward the upper end.
- Protein: 1.6-2.2 g/kg. Carbohydrates fuel training volume; fats at least 0.6-0.8 g/kg.
- Training: higher volume (14-20 hard sets per muscle per week), 4-6 days, progressive overload in the 6-15 rep range. Rest 2-3 minutes on compounds.
- Cardio: 2-3 short low-intensity sessions per week for heart health; it does not meaningfully interfere with gains at this dose.

## Cutting (fat loss while keeping muscle)
- Calorie deficit: roughly 300-500 kcal below maintenance (about 15-25%), aiming to lose 0.5-1% of body weight per week (Helms et al., 2014). Faster loss increases muscle loss risk.
- Protein: 2.0-2.4 g/kg to protect lean mass.
- Training: keep loads heavy to signal muscle retention; volume can drop about 20-30% (10-15 sets per muscle per week) as recovery is reduced. 3-5 days per week.
- Cardio and activity: 7,000-10,000 daily steps plus 2-4 sessions of low-to-moderate intensity cardio (20-40 min) or 1-2 short interval sessions. Avoid heavy interval work right before leg training.
- Rest periods can be slightly shorter (60-120 s) on accessories to save time, but keep 2-3 min on heavy compounds.

## Maintenance and body recomposition
- Calories at maintenance (TDEE). Protein 1.6-2.2 g/kg.
- Beginners, returning lifters and people with higher body fat can build muscle and lose fat simultaneously (recomposition) at maintenance calories with consistent progressive overload.
- Training: 3-5 days, moderate volume (10-14 sets per muscle per week). Muscle can be maintained with as little as one-third of previous volume if intensity stays high (Bickel et al., 2011).
- Cardio: meet the WHO guideline of 150-300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity per week.

## Hydration and recovery
Drink about 30-40 ml of water per kg of body weight daily, more on training days and in hot climates. Sleep 7-9 hours. Creatine monohydrate (3-5 g per day) is one of the most researched and effective supplements for strength and muscle gain.
